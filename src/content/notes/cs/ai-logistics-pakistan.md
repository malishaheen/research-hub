## Notes

### Is it being done?

Yes, but unevenly across sectors.

Pakistan's SME sector runs on tight margins, manual processes, and fragmented workflows. For these businesses, AI is treated less as a luxury and more as a survival tool, given the pain points already present: invoicing on paper, customer service handled entirely through WhatsApp, supply chains managed in spreadsheets.

On the logistics-tech side specifically, homegrown startups have already built AI/ML into daily operations:

- **Swyft Logistics** uses AI and machine learning to automate parcel sorting, cutting the process from hours to minutes, and to decide routing (which parcel goes to which side of the city).
- **Trax Logistics** runs a cloud-based delivery management system with API-driven bookings and integrations (Shopify, Magento plugins), built to stay stable even at high order volumes (50,000+ orders without downtime).
- **TPL Trakker** powers Bykea's app with Location-Based Service APIs, backed by a dataset covering 400 cities, 4.5 million geocoded addresses, and 600,000 km of road network -- the geographic data foundation that route-optimization AI depends on.

### Broader national context

Pakistan's IT and IT-enabled services exports reached $3.39 billion in July-March FY2025-26, a 20% year-on-year increase, with monthly exports hitting $413 million in March 2026. This signals a growing technical talent base that logistics AI efforts can draw from, even if it's not logistics-specific.

### Where it's NOT happening (or moving slowly)

Public-sector adoption lags well behind the private sector. Research specifically on AI in Pakistan's government-sector supply chain management identified bureaucracy as the crucial barrier to adoption across five key functions: demand management, procurement management, logistics management, disposal management, and risk management.

### Can it be done -- what the regional benchmark suggests

Asia-Pacific logistics providers already lead globally in AI maturity: 31% report success embedding AI across core operations, compared to just 14% in North America and 6% in Europe. This suggests the bottleneck isn't geography or regional readiness -- it's execution and data maturity. Nothing about Pakistan's structural position rules out logistics AI; the private sector is already proving that out in production.

### My takeaways / questions to explore further

- The gap between private-sector execution (Swyft, Trax, TPL) and public-sector paralysis (bureaucracy) is the most interesting fault line here -- worth exploring as its own topic.
- Most of what's live in Pakistan today is route optimization, parcel sorting, and LBS/mapping -- narrower than the "predictive, self-optimizing systems" being discussed globally (digital twins, agentic AI, autonomous replenishment). Worth tracking how far behind the frontier this puts Pakistan's logistics AI, and whether that gap is closing or widening.
- Potential angle for consulting/SME work: demand forecasting and inventory tools for distribution businesses running on spreadsheets -- this is explicitly called out as an underserved, high-pain segment.
